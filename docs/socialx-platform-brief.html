[ The definition ]

The sibling logic

socialX is the productized wedge. growX is full service and bespoke. socialX never drifts into custom agency scope, media buying, or strategy retainers, because blurring the two breaks both models. A buyer who wants that work gets routed to growX.

[ The problem it removes ]

The deal dies before the demo.

A HighLevel reseller sells trust before software. The first thing a prospect checks is the reseller's own social presence, and an invisible or amateur feed loses the deal before anyone books a call. The founder rarely sees it happen.

Nothing in their week is a content problem to them. It is a trust problem, a pipeline problem, and a time problem wearing a content costume.
Pain 01

No time, no system

Closing deals, onboarding clients, running support. Content falls off every week, and prospects notice the ghost account.

Pain 02

DIY kills positioning

Sunday night Canva posts look amateur next to real SaaS brands, and it caps what they can charge.

Pain 03

Generic SMM does not speak HighLevel

Freelancers who have never touched the platform produce fluffy, platform agnostic content that is useless for HL aware prospects.

Pain 04

Freelancer chaos

Three writers, two designers, missed deadlines. They end up managing people instead of growing the SaaS.

Who it refuses

The productized model only works when the wrong buyers stay out, so the refuse list is part of the product rather than a missed opportunity.

  • Large agencies wanting custom strategy retainers. They are buying growX's service, not socialX's product.
  • Non HighLevel shops. The entire library is HL native. Without the platform the value collapses and fulfillment breaks.
  • Micromanagers who want to art direct every post. The approval loop becomes the product and the economics die.
  • Buyers wanting SMM for their end clients. That is the future white label tier. Waitlist them, never sell it as available now.
  • Hobbyists without a live offer, and one off bargain hunters. Published pricing filters them by design.

[ How it runs ]

Three steps, then it just runs.

The client's work is front loaded into one intake and one approval per batch. Everything after that is the machine.

Step 01

Tell us about your brand

A 15 minute intake: logo, colors, voice, ICP, platforms, and restricted content manager access to the HL subaccount. Done once.

Step 02

We build your batch

Feature targeted posts pulled from the library and customized to the brand and the services the SaaS sells. Growth and Scale add custom posts and motion video.

Step 03

You approve, we schedule

Review and approve. It loads straight into the HL Social Planner and publishes on schedule. The client's job ends there.

Why resellers pick it over the alternatives

  • HighLevel only. Every library post is built around a real HL feature and written by people who live in the ecosystem, so clients skip the platform briefing entirely.
  • Scheduled, not delivered. Content lands scheduled in the client's HL Social Planner, not as a zip file they still have to post.
  • A customization ladder, stated honestly. Brand swap on Starter, full rewrite on Growth, bespoke on Scale. Starter is never oversold as bespoke.
  • Productized flat pricing. Three published tiers, self serve checkout, no proposals and no discovery call gate.
  • The library updates when HighLevel ships. Feature tags run as an independent axis across all pillars, so every affected post can be mass updated.
  • Built by the team behind GHL Video. 1000+ HighLevel clients served by the founding team.
[ The machine ]

What has to be true behind the tiers.

Four stages run the business: content production, marketing, sales and onboarding, and fulfillment. The library is the asset that makes the flat price work.

Library rules

  • Five content pillars with a default monthly mix: Feature Spotlight 30%, Education 25%, Pain Agitation 18%, Social Proof 15%, Promotional 12%.
  • Niche neutral defaults. Every library entry works for any client out of the box. Niche enters only at the customization stage.
  • growX CRM is the one demo brand, and it is real. Templates are built around it first, run on its live feed, then get customized per client.
  • Customer first structure. The hook opens on the reader's world, the HL feature is the quiet resolution in a dedicated middle beat, then outcome, then one CTA.
  • Phase 1 of the build covers 285 posts plus 30 motion scripts across all 27 HighLevel features, produced in horizontal waves so every feature is covered early.

Fulfillment phases

Client facing approval flow, template variable customization, and direct HL Social Planner API integration. This portal is socialX's long term moat, and it is the difference between a service that scales and one that caps out at the team's manual capacity.

Worth knowing

The live FAQ already tells clients they approve batches in a socialX dashboard and manage their subscription from a customer portal. Neither exists yet. The copy is describing Phase 3 while the business is running Phase 1.

[ The rules ]

How socialX speaks, and what it will not claim.

Direct, outcome focused, founder to founder. The reader is a HighLevel SaaS founder by default, so SaaS economics are never explained.

Customer first hook, the HL feature as the quiet resolution, then the outcome, then one CTA. Never lead with the product.
Copy rules

How it is written

Zero em dashes across every asset, internal or public, and no en dashes, middots, or ellipsis characters either. Exact casing on socialX, growX, GHL Video, HighLevel, and HL. No SaaS 101 explanations. Contractions are fine. No generic CTAs: name the action.

Words that repel

What loses the reader

Agency speak and brand storytelling fluff. The words affordable and cheap, which trigger the Fiverr scar rather than the wallet. Explaining MRR or churn. Hype and exclamation energy. Algorithm chasing talk. Any implied HighLevel affiliation.
